The difference

Every other tool starts when you open it.

This one was already working. Here is how a notice that lands before anybody is at a desk moves through it.

6:02 am
The board changed
The counselling site posted the Round 2 dates. Nobody was watching. The dashboard was.
6:02 am
You were told
Telegram and email, the same minute. You read it over breakfast instead of finding out at noon.
6:04 am
The draft existed
Caption, carousel and a creative brief — read back against the notice first, so it says only what the notice said.
9:15 am
You tapped approve
The only step that needed a person. It went out, and the live link wrote itself back into the sheet.
Three hours and thirteen minutes, and two minutes of it were yours. The rest happened while the office was shut.
Without this, that notice gets spotted mid‑morning, drafted after lunch, and posted once the attention has moved on.
01
Plan and run the work

Who is doing what, and where it is stuck.

The biggest part of the dashboard, and the part people have open all day. Every task — brief, owner, stage, due date, files, approvals — on one row. Edit in the grid, drag to a new date, and see at a glance which column everything is piling up in.

Managers

Marketing Hub

The master sheet. Filter it, group it, edit inline, and open any task to its full history. A bottleneck panel shows where work has stopped moving.

Everyone

My Workspace

Your own queue instead of the whole board — today's tasks, what you have claimed, what is waiting on you, and your hours.

Managers

Team & permissions

Who is on the team, what each person may do, and ready-made roles so a new joiner is set up in a minute rather than an afternoon.

Everyone

Content review

Work waiting for approval, in one list, with the brief and the creative side by side instead of in two tabs.

Everyone

Notifications

Twenty approvals read as one line. Opening it takes you to exactly those tasks, not to a feed you have to search.

Managers

Team Command

The day across the whole team — who is in, what each person is on, and what has not been picked up.

02
Find something worth saying, and draft it

The blank page, mostly filled in.

Four tabs that between them answer "what should we post" before anyone has to invent an answer — then turn the chosen thing into a draft that is tied to its source.

Watchers

Counselling boards and council sites, checked every half hour. When a page changes you hear about it instead of going to look.

Content Radar

Forums, review sites, question boards and the news — wherever students are actually talking about this.

Content Studio

An item becomes a carousel, a reel script, a caption and a poster brief — read back against the source first, so it says only what the source said.

Briefings

What competitors did and what is trending, already waiting when you open the tab.

03
Put it out

One row per post. Four platforms. Nothing goes without your tap.

Schedule once and it reaches Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and YouTube at the time you set — images, carousels, reels, video. The live link writes itself back into the sheet, so nobody has to go and fetch it.

Scheduler

Pick the time, pick the accounts, attach the creative. A copyright check and a caption suggestion before it goes.

Content & publishing calendar

The month at a glance — what is planned, what is approved, what already went out, and the gaps.

Community Broadcast

Message the whole WhatsApp list at once, with a checker that reads your draft and tells you whether it will come back approved.

Approve from Telegram

The draft and the creative arrive with two buttons. Tap approve and it is scheduled; tap send back and it returns to the writer with your note.

Posts, reels, stories

What has already gone out, with how each one did, instead of scrolling the app to remember.

Recycle bin

Deleted a task by accident? It is recoverable for a while, not gone the instant someone mis-clicks.

04
Read what happened

Five platforms over the same period, in the same place.

The second biggest part of the dashboard. Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, YouTube and the website — one date range, one screen, and a written note on what to do about it rather than a chart you have to interpret yourself.

Analytics

Reach, engagement, followers and profile visits per channel and combined, with the previous period beside it.

Audience

Who is actually following — city, age, when they are online — so posting times stop being guesswork.

Competitors

Side by side, tracked over time, with a plain note on what changed since you last looked.

Website & SEO

Traffic, search terms and behaviour for the site, next to the social numbers instead of in a separate login.

Ads

Campaigns, spend and cost per lead, so paid and organic can be compared on one screen.

Reports

A written monthly report you can send, generated from the same numbers rather than retyped into a deck.

05
Follow it through to an enrolment

The half most marketing tools stop short of.

A post is not the point; a student is. Leads arrive from comments, DMs, forms and ads, get assigned to a counsellor, and are tracked to what they became — in the same product that published the post.

Leads

Everything inbound in one list — who, from where, when, and whether anyone has touched it yet.

Counsellors

How many each person holds and how much of it has actually been worked, so the load is visible rather than assumed.

Revenue

What came in, by counsellor, source and programme, each figure linking back to the row it was typed in.

Inbox & comments

DMs and comments across the accounts in one place, so nothing sits unanswered because it was on the wrong app.

Transfers & unassigned

Hand a lead over without losing its history, and see immediately what nobody has picked up.

Per-day tracker

Leads by day against who was on, so a quiet week is a fact rather than an argument.

+
Underneath all of it

Sixteen jobs that run whether anyone is in or not.

The imports, the notice-board checks, the snapshots, the publishing queue and the morning health check all run on a schedule — from 6am to midnight. Between midnight and six, deliberately nothing: no board publishes at 3am and nobody is reading, so every call in that window would be spend with no reader.

Diagnostics

One page saying whether every connection is alive, repairing what it safely can, and flagging anything expiring before it breaks.

Workflows

Every scheduled job with its last run, its schedule and what it costs — so a job that quietly stopped is visible the same day.

Search across everything

One box that finds a post, a report or a contact without you guessing which tab it was in.

Questions

The ones people ask.

Will it post something without asking me?

No. It watches, drafts and schedules. Publishing waits for a person — from the dashboard or from Telegram.

Does it replace the sheets we already keep?

It reads from them and keeps them current, so nobody has to move. The master sheet is the same work, editable in one grid.

Do I have to use all of it?

No. Most people live in two tabs — their own queue and the calendar. The rest is there when you need it, and permissions mean you only see the parts that are yours.

What happens overnight?

Deliberately nothing, between midnight and 6am. Anything that lands in that window is drafted and waiting when the day starts.

What happens when something breaks?

It says so. Every job reports its last run, its errors and its cost, and the morning check flags anything expiring before it stops working.

Open it and start with your own queue.

Everything else is still there when you want it. Nothing goes out without you.
