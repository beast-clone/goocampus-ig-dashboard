How it works

Four steps, and the
first three run themselves.

A person still approves anything that goes out. Everything before that happens whether or not the office is open.

01

It watches

Notice boards, mentions, competitors and your own numbers — on a schedule, with nobody at a keyboard. Weekends included; overnight it deliberately rests, and whatever lands is waiting at six.

02

It tells you

In the dashboard, in your inbox, and on Telegram — so you hear about it even when you are nowhere near a computer.

03

It writes

One tap turns the item into a brief and then a draft — caption, carousel, script, creative — checked against the original so nothing is made up.

Scheduled for 7:30 pm
04

You approve, it publishes

Approve from the dashboard or straight from Telegram. It goes out at the time you set and tells you, with the live link.

Approve from anywhere

The office does not
have to be open.

When something lands, the draft and the creative arrive on Telegram with two buttons. Tap approve and it is scheduled; tap send back and it returns to the writer with your note.

Nothing goes out without that tap. The system does the waiting; you make the decision.

Questions

The ones
people ask.

Will it post something without asking me?

No. It watches, drafts and schedules, but publishing waits for a person to approve — from the dashboard or from Telegram.

Does it replace the sheets we already keep?

It reads from them and keeps them up to date, so nobody has to move. The master sheet in the Hub is the same work, just editable in one grid.

What happens when nobody is in the office?

The watching and the drafting carry on. You get the alert wherever you are, and can approve from your phone without opening the dashboard.

How does it avoid writing things that are not true?

Every draft is tied to the item it came from and checked against it before the copy is written. Where a fact is missing it is left as a blank for a person to fill, not guessed.

Why does it check messages before they are submitted?

Because a rejected message costs a day. The checker reads the draft, says how likely it is to be accepted and why, and shows a version that would pass next to the one you wrote.

Open it and start
with one notice.

Pick a board to watch. The next time it changes, the dashboard will have the draft waiting before anyone has noticed.
